# Ship Types: Other Games, the Period, and What Corsair Is Missing

Scope: what ships other pirate games use, which types really sailed the Caribbean in 1650-1720, and which of them Corsair should add or rename. It builds on `naval-combat-history.md` section 1, which covers our twelve classes against history; this file does not repeat that table and refers to it as [NCH]. Combat roles refer to `../design/combat-model.md` [CM].

**How reliable the numbers are.** [S#] marks a source fetched or searched for this report (list at the end). [gen] marks standard figures from the naval-history literature that I did not re-check this session. Game data from the Pirates! manuals and the Akella source files are primary sources (the manual itself or the game's own ship definitions).

## 1. Ships in other games

### 1.1 Sid Meier's Pirates! (1987), and Pirates! Gold (1993)

Nine types. The 1987 manual lists a maximum and a *typical* armament and crew for each, which is how one hull serves as both trader and warship [S1]. Rig matters: the three fore-and-aft types (pinnace, sloop, barque) are best on a broad beam reach; the rest are square-rigged and best on a broad reach or running [S1][S2].

**What to take from it:** Pirates! has three fast oared small craft *below* the sloop. The pinnace the user remembers is the 2004 small family: a tiny, oared, fore-and-aft boat with 6-60 men and 8-12 guns, faster than a sloop into the wind. Corsair's smallest ship is the sloop, so that whole rung is missing.

**What to take from it:** Akella's *pinnace* is the opposite of Pirates!'s: a class-3, ship-rigged, 18-24-gun **merchant** with the biggest hold in its class. That is the Dutch *pinas*, a race-built three-master the size of a small frigate [S9]. Both games are "right" about a different century or nation (section 2). Akella also leans on Mediterranean and later types (xebec, polacre, tartane, lugger, corvette, schooner) that a historian calls "a compote from the types of sailing ships": corvettes are early 19th century, there are no three-masted brigs, caravels are misnamed [S8].

### 1.4 Assassin's Creed IV: Black Flag (2013)

Five enemy classes, qualitative only (I found no reliable numeric source) [S10][S11]: **gunboat** (very fast, very weak, few guns; ammunition and wanted-level fodder), **schooner** (fast, weak hull, fire barrels), **brig** (balanced; the player's Jackdaw is one), **frigate** (heavy broadsides, wide turning circle), **man-of-war** (heavy armour, mortars, huge broadsides, slow). Legendary ships are boss variants. Lesson: each class has *one weapon or trick* the player has to answer (fire barrels astern, mortars at range), not only bigger numbers.

### 1.5 Naval Action

Partial (the wiki was unreachable). Naval Action is mostly 1750-1815; ships by rate run from 7th (cutter, Lynx, Privateer, Pickle) to 1st [S12]. Its only pre-1720 designs are the *Wasa* (Swedish, 1628, a 64-gun 3rd rate in game), *Wapen von Hamburg* (Hamburg convoy ship, versions of 1669, 1686 and 1720; 68 guns in game) and *Ingermanland* (Russian, 1715 design, 62 guns, 4th rate) [S12][S13]. Not a useful source of small period types.

## 3. Gap analysis against Corsair's twelve

### 3.1 Naming fixes (existing classes)

| Class | Issue | Fix |

### 3.2 The pinnace: pick one meaning

Four things share the name:

1. **Pirates! pinnace** (the one the user remembers): a tiny, oared, fore-and-aft boat with 8-12 guns, the rung below the sloop [S1][S4].
2. **English pinnace c.1600-1670**: about 100 t, 5-16 guns, two or three masts, light warship and tender [S9].
3. **Dutch pinas**: a ship-rigged three-master, small galleon to small frigate size [S9].
4. **Akella pinnace**: a class-3 big merchant (that is meaning 3) [S5][S6].

**Recommendation:** in 1660, the *role* of the Pirates! pinnace (small, oared, crammed with men, rows into the wind) was filled by the **barque longue** and the **periagua**. Add those under their period names. Use "pinnace" for meaning 2: a light square-rigged cruiser with sweeps, fast off the wind, the buccaneer's first "real ship". If the familiar name matters more than the history, call the barque longue "pinnace" in the UI instead. Either way, there is one class, not two.

### 3.3 Proposed additions, in Corsair's units

Method: each type is interpolated between the two existing entries named in "Between", using the ratios in `ships.json`: minCrew ≈ 1.2-2x guns, maxCrew ≈ 7-9x guns for warships, and price following hull (45 → 3,000, 55 → 4,500, 85 → 6,500, 130 → 14,000, 180 → 30,000). Speed and turn are on our 1-10 scale. `draft` 0 means a craft that crosses reefs. Stats are first guesses for the battle probe, not tuned. Eras are historical years. The `eras` field in `ships.json` holds start-era tokens that stop at `"1680"`, so a type that arrives mid-game (galley-frigate 1676, bomb ketch 1682, snow 1690, schooner 1716) needs a year gate the schema does not have yet; until then, map it to `"1680"` or gate it by in-game year.

### 3.4 What each new type adds to combat

- **Oars (periagua, half-galley, barque longue, pinnace, galley-frigate).** A slow, fixed speed in any wind direction, including dead into it and in a flat calm. That matters a lot in Caribbean lulls [NCH]. In a calm an oared boat chooses the range and angle against a becalmed ship: it lies on her bow or stern, where her broadside cannot bear, and boards. Answer: stern and bow chasers, swivels, keep a breeze. The *Adventure Galley* figures (14 knots sail, 3 oars [S35]) give the ratio: oars ≈ a fifth of top sail speed.
- **Swivels (periagua, half-galley).** Grape at close range: kills crew, not hull. Makes a boarding craft dangerous to small merchants and gives the defender a reason to keep a big crew.
- **Shallow draught (draft 0).** Crosses reefs and bars that larger ships cannot (Pirates! 1987 already did this [S1]). Escape over a reef, or ambush from a lagoon.
- **Bow chasers and one big gun (half-galley).** It fires along its heading, so it attacks bow-on, the reverse of the broadside duel.
- **Lug and lateen rig (barque longue, half-galley).** Points higher than a square-rigger; "excellent for chasing or escaping to windward" [NCH S1]. A third polar between `polar.square` and `polar.fore_aft`.
- **Mortar (bomb ketch).** Indirect fire at long range and slow reload, the Black Flag man-of-war's trick [S10]. Mostly a coastal-fort and town weapon; use sparingly.
- **Light frigate.** Not a new mechanic. It closes the biggest gap in the ladder: there is nothing between the 18-gun brig and the 32-gun frigate (14,000 gold).
- **Galley-frigate.** A frigate that cannot be becalmed: a strong rung 4 privateer flagship (Kidd), and a historically sound pirate hunter.

### 3.5 Where they sit on the career ladder

[CM] starts the player in a brig (rung 1), so small craft are not the default start. They fit as:

| Rung [CM] | New types and their job |
|---|---|
| 0 (optional "buccaneer start", or a prologue) | Periagua or barque longue: the Morgan and Bellamy start [S17][S18]. Take a sloop or bark by boarding, then sell up to a brig |
| 1-2 (Jamaica and Leewards coasts) | **Enemies:** pirate periaguas and barques longues that ambush in calms; Spanish half-galleys (guarda costa) off Cuba and Puerto Rico. **Prey:** ketches and pinks, small and easy |
| 2-3 (Windward Passage, Hispaniola) | Pinnace and barque longue as cheap fast hulls for a raiding player; ketch as a coastal trader |
| 3-4 (Spanish Main) | **Light frigate** as the bridge from brig to frigate; galley-frigate as a privateer flagship (1676+); bomb ketches with navy squadrons in wartime |
| 4-5 (treasure routes) | Fourth rate (renamed 44) and wartime-only third rates as escorts; fireships as the player's answer to them (Maracaibo [S38]) |

### 3.6 Recommended order

1. **Light frigate**: no new mechanics, closes the brig-to-frigate gap.
2. **Barque longue**: the period's real small raider, and the pinnace role the user remembers. Needs `oars` and `polar.lug`.
3. **Half-galley and periagua** as enemies (guarda costa, calm-weather ambush). Reuse `oars`; add `swivels`.
4. **Ketch and pink** as small prey and traders: data only.
5. **Galley-frigate** once oars exist.
6. Renames: brig → snow (1680+ eras), barque → bark, royal sloop → Bermuda sloop, ship of the line (44) → fourth rate; sloop eras from 1660.
7. Later: pinnace (English sense), bomb ketch, wartime third rate, schooner (1716+), fireship action.
