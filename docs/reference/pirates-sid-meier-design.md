# Sid Meier on Pirates!: design decisions

Collected 2026-10-09 from interviews and talks.

Primary sources: IGN Retro Developer Commentary (Sid playing the 2004 remake), Rouse *Game Design: Theory & Practice*, Metro 2015, GDC 2010, PAX/EGX 2020, Video Game History Hour, Firaxis Designer Diaries. Retro Gamer #38 is not available online.

## Summary

Sid built Pirates! (1987) from the pirate-movie fantasy, not from a genre. The cool stuff: sailing, sword fights, ship battles, towns, romance, the evil Spaniard. Each part got the style that fit it. Action scenes stayed short so players never lost track of the career (the center of gravity). No scurvy and no "bad pirate" grit. Parser-style towns became short menus. The 2004 remake kept the core and added more hand-holding, turn-based land battles, and dancing and sneaking minigames (reviewers liked those less).

## Fantasy

- Errol Flynn was the inspiration; the player is a swashbuckling hero, and action sequences tie the story segments together ([2004 fan-site interview](https://spillhistorie.no/2024/04/24/interview-with-sid-meier-from-2004/)).
- "there's gotta be swordfighting, there's gotta be ship battles… no scurvy, we didn't put in any of the bad pirate stuff" ([Metro 2015](https://metro.co.uk/2015/03/12/sid-meier-interview-a-lifelong-strategy-from-pirates-to-starships-5099845/)).
- Fun first, research after; children's books were used to capture the shared pirate myth (Metro 2015).
- A romantic movie view of piracy, not a historical one ([IGN Retro Developer Commentary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eK4kkxFOi8A)).
- A black curly mustache tells you who the villain is, saving millions in backstory ([GDC 2010](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bY7aRJE-oOY)).
- Games give players an aspirational role they don't have in real life ([PAX 2020](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TU42GQoXBHU)).
- Memoir quote via Retroist: tired of hyperrealism ([Retroist](https://www.retroist.com/p/sid-meiers-pirates)).

## Genre mix and the Covert Action rule

- No existing genre fit pirates, so the game mixes real-time sailing, menu towns, and action duels. Better one great game than two good ones; minigames must be simple enough that the player doesn't lose track of the main game (Rouse).
- The center of gravity is the career; battles and duels are short interludes (IGN commentary around 18:00; PAX 2020).
- The "Pirates! Rule": just do it right ([RPS on PAX/EGX](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/civilizations-sid-meier-talked-to-rps-all-about-his-brilliant-career-at-pax-x-egx)).

## Towns

- A reaction against text parsers ("LOOK AT THE STICK") (Metro 2015).
- The computer gives three choices and you pick one ([Video Game History Hour](https://gamehistory.org/ep-8-sid-meiers-episode-of-the-video-game-history-hour/)).
- Only the key options in town (IGN commentary around 6:20).

## Sailing and combat

- Wind is fundamental. In the remake the sails move with the wind, which Sid is proud of (IGN).
- Cheat the scale: big and colorful serves the storytelling (IGN).
- Non-lethal: sailors jump overboard before a ship sinks; duel speed scales for difficulty and forgiving fun (IGN).
- Land battles were reinvented as turn-based after real-time didn't feel right ([Designer Diary #4](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/sid-meiers-pirates-designer-diary-4/1100-6109151/)). Sid apologised for the dancing.

## Career and living world

- A whole lifetime as a pirate; the player is the star of their own movie (PAX 2020; VGHH).
- The world continues on its own: rival captains, economies hurt by raids, colonies changing hands (IGN).
- Saving only in port means you live with the consequences of a voyage (GDC 2010 around 42:44).
- The player makes the big decisions; the designer makes the small ones (Rouse).

## Cut or avoided

- No parser, no item fiddling, no gratuitous killing, no long minigames.
- Briggs, [Designer Diary #2](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/sid-meiers-pirates-designer-diary-2/1100-6102516/): no plague, dysentery, barnacles, or rotting food; capture means jail and then escape. The filter is "is it fun" plus "does it fit the heroic world".

## Corsair takeaways

1. Career plus world map is the main game; battles, duels, and social scenes stay short.
2. The shared pirate myth is free onboarding; put research into texture, not grit.
3. Failure is a career setback, not game over.
4. Tie meta systems to the fiction (for example port-only saves).
5. A few strong town choices, not chores.
6. The world runs without the player.
7. Wind is spice; long upwind slogs hurt.
8. New minigames (dancing, stealth) are risks.
